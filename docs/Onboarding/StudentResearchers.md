# Undergraduate and High School Researchers

Welcome! Undergraduate and high school students are an important part of the lab. This page
explains how research works for you and which parts of this manual matter most.

## What to expect

- You'll work on a well-defined piece of a lab project, usually with day-to-day guidance from a
  **graduate student or postdoc mentor** and regular check-ins with the PI.
- You'll learn real research skills: working with climate and crop/ecosystem model data, Python,
  the command line, FARM, Git, and presenting results.
- You're welcome at the **weekly lab meeting**. Attending is one of the best ways to learn what
  research looks like.

## Before you start

- Agree with your mentor and the PI on your **project goals**, a **weekly schedule**, and how you'll
  communicate (usually Slack).
- Work through the parts of the [First-Week Checklist](FirstWeekChecklist.md) that apply to you:
  Slack, Box, GitHub, and, if your project needs it, a FARM account.

## Undergraduates

- **Research for credit**: you can often earn units through a special-study course (typically
  numbered 99 for lower division or 199 for upper division) in the relevant department. Ask the
  PI before enrolling.
- **Paid positions and funding**: ask the PI whether a paid position is possible, and look at the
  [Undergraduate Research Center](https://urc.ucdavis.edu) for funding, programs, and the annual
  Undergraduate Research, Scholarship and Creative Activities Conference.
- **Senior thesis or honors projects**: discuss early with the PI if you're interested.

## High school students

- Participation is arranged with the PI in advance and must follow UC Davis rules for minors
  (under 18) taking part in university activities. The PI will tell you which forms and approvals
  are needed (for example, parent or guardian consent) **before you start**.

- Work in the lab or on campus only at times agreed with your mentor.

## At the end of your project

- Present your work at lab meeting, and consider a poster at a campus research conference.
- Push all your code to GitHub and document where your data and results are.
- Ask your mentor and the PI about letters of recommendation while your work is fresh.
